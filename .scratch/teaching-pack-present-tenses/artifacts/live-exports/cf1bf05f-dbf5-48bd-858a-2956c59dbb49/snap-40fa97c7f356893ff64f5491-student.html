practice Section 1: Present Tense Identification Identify the correct present tense form Read each sentence carefully and decide which present tense form is used or required. Vietnamese has no verb conjugation, so do NOT rely on the verb shape alone — look at time markers (every day, now, already, for two years, since 2010) and aspectual clues. Quick Reference Simple Present = habits/facts (every day, usually, always). Present Continuous = action now (now, right now, at the moment). Present Perfect = past action with present relevance (already, yet, just, ever, never). Present Perfect Continuous = duration from past to now (for, since, how long). practice Section 2: Error Correction Using Inverse Thinking Apply the inverse-thinking workflow Inverse-Thinking Workflow Step 1 — Spot the broken verb. Step 2 — Ask: 'What tense does the TIME MARKER demand?' Step 3 — Ask: 'What did Vietnamese tempt me to write instead?' Step 4 — Rewrite using the correct English structure. Always write the correction AND a short justification. Error: 'She don't like coffee.' Choose the correct version and identify the interference. She doesn't like coffee. — Vietnamese has no -s on third-person verbs. She not like coffee. — Vietnamese negative uses 'không' before the verb. She isn't like coffee. — Negative needs 'not' after the auxiliary. She doesn't likes coffee. — Keep both -s and 'not' for emphasis. Error: 'I am knowing the answer.' Choose the correct rewrite. I know the answer. — 'know' is a stative verb, no continuous form. I am knowing the answer for ten minutes. — Add 'for' to make it continuous. I have been knowing her since primary school. — 'Have been knowing' is allowed. I am know the answer. — Drop the -ing on 'know'. Error: 'They has been working here since January.' Choose the correct rewrite. They have been working here since January. — 'They' takes 'have', not 'has'. They has worked here since January. — Use perfect, not perfect continuous. They are working here since January. — Replace the auxiliary. They has been working here since January. — Plural 'they' keeps 'has'. Error: 'I have been studied English for three years.' Choose the correct rewrite. I have been studying English for three years. — After 'have been', use V-ing. I have studied English for three years. — Simple perfect replaces the duration focus. I am studying English for three years. — Use continuous for duration. I have study English for three years. — Drop the -ed on the main verb. Error: 'I am playing football when my friend called me yesterday.' Choose the correct rewrite. I was playing football when my friend called me yesterday. — 'yesterday' triggers past. I am playing football when my friend calls me yesterday. — Keep present, drop 'yesterday'. I have been playing football when my friend called me. — Use perfect continuous. I am playing football when my friend is calling me. — Change 'called' to -ing.

practice Section 5: Mixed Application Higher-order discrimination These items require you to choose between two closely related tenses. Read both clauses before answering. Choose the more natural sentence and explain why in one sentence. I have been knowing her since childhood. I have known her since childhood. Both are equally natural. Neither is grammatical. Choose the sentence that correctly matches time marker and verb. I have lived here since five years. I am living here since 2019. I have been living here for five years. I live here since 2019. Which sentence BEST expresses a complaint about an action that began in the past and is STILL happening now? You make too much noise. You are making too much noise. You have made too much noise. You have been making too much noise for an hour!

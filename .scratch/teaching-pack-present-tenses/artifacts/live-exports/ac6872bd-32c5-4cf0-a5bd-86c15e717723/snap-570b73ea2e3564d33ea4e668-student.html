Present Tenses – Inverse Thinking Worksheet for Vietnamese Learners

introduction Worksheet Instructions and Key Rules This worksheet uses inverse thinking: instead of starting with rules, you start with errors that Vietnamese learners often make. For each item, first identify the mistake, then explain the rule it breaks, and finally write the correct sentence. Read every sentence carefully and pay attention to time markers (every day, since, for, right now, etc.) and verb types (action vs. stative). Quick Reference: Form and Typical Use of the Four Present Tenses Three Common Vietnamese-Learner Errors 1. Using stative verbs (know, like, want, need, belong, love) in continuous form — e.g., 'I am knowing him' is wrong. 2. Forgetting the third-person singular -s/-es in Present Simple — e.g., 'She go to school' should be 'She goes to school.' 3. Mixing up Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous, or using 'since' with a duration and 'for' with a point in time.
practice Part A – Error Identification (Multiple Choice) Read each sentence carefully. Choose A, B, C, or D that correctly identifies the type of error in the underlined part. Circle your choice. Error in: "I am knowing him for two years." What rule is being broken? Error in: "She go to school every day." What rule is being broken? Error in: "I have been lived in Hanoi since 2020." What rule is being broken?
practice Part B – Error Correction (Short Answer) Rewrite each sentence so that it is grammatically correct. Then, in one short sentence, name the rule you applied (e.g., 'Since + point in time → for + duration' or 'Habitual action → Present Simple'). Correct this sentence and explain the rule: 'I have lived here since five years.' Correct this sentence and explain the rule: 'They are watching TV every evening.' Correct this sentence and explain the rule: 'He has been went to Hanoi last week.' Correct this sentence and explain the rule: 'My brother don't like coffee, but he is wanting some tea.'
practice Part C – Paragraph Analysis Read the paragraph about Mai's daily routine. For each underlined verb phrase, write the tense name in column 3 and a brief justification in column 4 (cite the time marker, the verb type, or both). Paragraph: 'Every morning, Mai (1) gets up at 6 a.m. Right now, she (2) is studying for her English test. She (3) has lived in Hanoi for five years. She (4) has been studying English since 2020. After class, she usually (5) walks home with her friends.'
practice Part D – Mini-Dialogue Creation (Pair Work) Work with a partner. Together, write a 10-line dialogue about a Vietnamese student's daily routine. Your dialogue must correctly use all four present tenses at least once. Label each line with the speaker (A or B). Distribution Suggestion Lines 1–2: Present Simple (habits). Lines 3–4: Present Continuous (now / at the moment). Lines 5–6: Present Perfect (with for/since/already/just). Lines 7–8: Present Perfect Continuous (with for/since, focus on duration). Lines 9–10: any mix, but each line must be grammatically correct.

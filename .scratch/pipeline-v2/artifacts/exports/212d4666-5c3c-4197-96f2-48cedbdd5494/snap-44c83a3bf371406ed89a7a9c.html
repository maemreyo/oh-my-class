Introduction to the Water Cycle

What is the Water Cycle?

The water cycle, also called the hydrologic cycle, describes how water moves continuously between Earth's surface, atmosphere, and underground reservoirs. Water changes state as it moves, driven by energy from the Sun and the force of gravity.

Drivers of the Water Cycle

What drives each step?

Solar Energy

Provides latent heat for evaporation and transpiration.

Cooling Air

Warm moist air rises, expands, and cools to its dew point, causing condensation.

Gravity

Pulls water droplets down as precipitation and pulls water downhill as runoff.

Forms of Precipitation

Match precipitation type to conditions

Cặp từPhân biệt nhanh
RainForms when cloud droplets coalesce and fall as liquid; temperatures stay above freezing throughout the cloud.
SnowForms when temperatures are below freezing throughout the cloud, allowing ice crystals to grow and fall.
SleetForms when snowflakes melt partially in a warm layer then refreeze into ice pellets before reaching the ground.
HailForms in strong thunderstorms with strong updrafts; layered ice balls grow as they are carried up and down through supercooled water.

Human Impact on the Water Cycle

How humans change the cycle

Cặp từPhân biệt nhanh
DeforestationRemoves trees that release water vapor via transpiration, reducing atmospheric moisture and local precipitation.
UrbanizationReplaces permeable soil with impermeable pavement, increasing runoff and reducing infiltration, which can lead to more flooding and less groundwater recharge.

Warm-up: Sharing a Pizza Fairly

Warm-up: Sharing a Pizza Fairly

Imagine a pizza cut into 8 equal slices shared among 4 friends. How many slices does each friend get? Talk with a partner about why the slices must be the same size. This is the heart of fractions: we split a whole into equal parts and count how many we have.

Equal parts mean every piece is the same size and shape. If pieces are different sizes, we cannot write a fair fraction.

Quick recall: share one example of something you have divided equally at home or school — sharing candy, cutting a sandwich, splitting play time.

What Is a Fraction?

Defining a Fraction

A fraction is a way to name part of a whole. It is written as one number on top of another, like 3/4. The bottom number is the denominator: it tells how many equal parts the whole is divided into. The top number is the numerator: it tells how many of those equal parts we are talking about.

Use visual fraction models: a circle, rectangle, or number line split into equal pieces. Count the shaded pieces to find the numerator. Count all equal pieces to find the denominator.

When the numerator equals the denominator, the fraction equals exactly 1 whole.

Comparing Fractions with Like Denominators

Comparing Fractions with Like Denominators

When two fractions have the same denominator, they are divided into the same size pieces. The fraction with the larger numerator has more pieces, so it is the larger fraction.

Adding Fractions with Like Denominators

Adding Fractions with Like Denominators

To add fractions with the same denominator, keep the denominator the same and add the numerators. The pieces are already the same size, so we are simply combining how many we have.

Keep the bottom, add the tops: 1/6 + 2/6 = (1 + 2)/6 = 3/6.
